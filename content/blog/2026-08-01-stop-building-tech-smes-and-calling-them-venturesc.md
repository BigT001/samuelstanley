I’m sick of reading post-mortems that sound like a script. "We failed to raise additional funding, so we're shutting down." That line is everywhere right now, and honestly? It’s a massive cop-out.

Not because raising money isn't hard—sapa hits hard in this ecosystem, trust me. But because we've conditioned ourselves to treat Venture Capital as the *only* validation that a software product is worth building.

![Laptop screen showing lines of code](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555066931-4365d14bab8c?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)

## You Built a Business, Not a Moonshot

Here is the cold truth: 80% of the software being built in Nigeria right now doesn't need VC money.

If you are building an inventory management app for spare parts dealers in Onitsha, or a booking portal for inter-state buses floating around Owerri, you are building a tech-enabled SME. And there is zero shame in that. In fact, it's brilliant. It solves real problems for real people who have cash in hand.

The problem starts when you take that idea, package it like a Silicon Valley moonshot, spin up an over-engineered Kubernetes cluster on AWS that costs $2,000 a month, hire six growth leads in a Gbagada workstation, and then panic when you can't hit 10x growth every single quarter.

You didn't fail because the VC funds dried up. You failed because you built a normal business with an unsustainable operating model.

## The Overhead Monster vs. Clean Execution

When I sit down to write code for a new product, my first thought isn't "how do I make this look like a unicorn?" It's "how cheap can I run this infrastructure until customer revenue pays for my server bills?"

We spend so much time perfecting pitch decks that we forget how to actually ship lean code that makes money.

- **Your tech stack is burning your runway:** Why are you spinning up three microservices, Kafka pipelines, and AWS Lambda functions for an app that has 500 active users? A clean monolith running Node.js or Go with PostgreSQL on a $12 server will easily do the job.
- **Monetize from day one:** If your customer in Akure or Jos won't pay ₦2,000 a month for your software, you don't have a growth engine problem. You have a product value problem.
- **The "No gree for anybody" approach to overhead:** Cut unnecessary SaaS tools. Stop paying for expensive analytics dashboards when a quick SQL query or a custom 20-line script gets you the same data.

![Data graphs showing business growth](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551288049-bebda4e38f71?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)

## We Need Debt, Cash-Flow, and Local Angels

The real crisis isn't that foreign VC money is drying up in Africa. The real crisis is that we haven't built proper alternative financing models for software builders.

Where are the revenue-based financing options? Where are the local angels who actually understand cash flow instead of just chasing a 100x return that rarely happens in our market?

Until those options mature, as builders, we have to change our mindset. We can't keep sitting around waiting for checks from London or San Francisco to save us every time the market shifts.

Build simple things that work. Sell them directly to people who need them. Keep the stack light, stay cash-flow positive, and let VC money be an extra boost if it comes—not your oxygen tank.
