Most people remember the 2021 playbook: promise someone that if they learn HTML, CSS, and basic JavaScript in 12 weeks, they’ll immediately land a $3,000/month remote role from their bedroom in Akure or Enugu. 

We all know how that turned out. The entry-level junior developer market got brutally saturated, AI tools changed how boilerplate code gets written, and thousands of folks who spent their savings on data bundles were left stranded with certificates nobody asked to see.

That’s why GetBundi's latest pivot with their TechSis 2026 cohort caught my attention. 

They’re onboarding 3,000 women for a four-month run. But instead of just throwing another sea of React tutorials at them, the curriculum is focusing on high-leverage digital execution: data analytics, content creation, digital marketing, and video editing.

![Workspace setup with laptop](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1517694712282-14f494bc6f0e?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)

### The Shift to Immediate Utility

As a builder, I love pure software engineering. But as someone running products and hiring talent, I can tell you that a solid technical video editor or a sharp data analyst who can build clean dashboards is often ten times harder to find right now than another junior web developer.

When you teach someone video editing, performance marketing, or analytics, the feedback loop to earning money is dramatically shorter:
- A local business in Owerri needs product videos that don’t look amateur.
- A creator in the UK needs a fast editor who understands TikTok retention curves.
- An e-commerce brand needs someone to clean up their Shopify analytics and track ad spend.

These aren't hypothetical four-year computer science problems. They are practical, service-based digital skills that a hungry learner with decent internet and an average laptop can turn into real freelancing income within six months.

### The Real Friction: Infrastructure and Retention

Scaling an online cohort from 500 in 2023 to 3,000 in 2026 sounds amazing on a press release. But anyone who has tried running a remote workshop in Nigeria knows the ground reality. 

Your biggest competitor isn’t a rival platform; it’s the national grid collapsing on a Tuesday evening, expensive 4G subscriptions running out mid-video upload, and the sheer mental fatigue of battling fuel queues to power a generator just to finish a four-hour capstone project.

![Tracking progress and data](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1551288049-bebda4e38f71?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop)

The curriculum design has to account for this. Asynchronous lessons, low-bandwidth video delivery, and project-based assignments that don’t require streaming 10GB of video assets every day are what make or break completion rates in this environment.

### Placement is the Only Metric That Counts

GetBundi is tying this cohort to a pipeline with Doballi, a remote talent marketplace based in Dubai. That’s the piece that matters.

Nobody needs another PDF certificate to hang on their wall. The only metric an edtech startup should care about is: *how many of these 3,000 women actually get paid work within 90 days of finishing?*

If you teach a woman in Jos or Abeokuta how to use CapCut, Premiere, SQL, or Google Analytics, and then hand her a direct link to remote gig platforms, you aren’t just running a social impact campaign. You’re building a decentralized workforce that brings foreign exchange directly into local households.

I'm keeping an eye on how this cohort executes over the next four months. If they pull off good completion and placement rates at a 3,000-person scale, it’ll be a solid blueprint for how practical tech education should work across the continent.
